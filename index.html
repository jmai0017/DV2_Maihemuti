Melbourne After Dark

Food Accessibility in Melbourne's Inner & Outer Suburbs

A data-driven analysis of late-night food venue distribution and accessibility patterns across Melbourne's metropolitan area.

Where Food Venues Are Located

Melbourne's food landscape is highly concentrated in the CBD and inner suburbs. This map reveals the geographic clustering of food venues across the metropolitan area. Dense bubbles indicate high venue concentration, with clear patterns emerging between central business districts and suburban areas.

Food Venue Density Across Districts

This visualization ranks CLUE small areas by their restaurant and cafe counts. The CBD dominates with the highest density, followed by inner suburbs like Carlton, Fitzroy, and South Yarra. Outer suburban areas show significantly lower counts, raising questions about food accessibility equity. The log scale helps reveal differences across the full range of densities.

Types of Food Venues by Area

Different food business types dominate different areas. Cafes are prevalent across most suburbs, while restaurants concentrate in inner-city and CBD locations. Takeaway services show more even distribution, serving suburban areas with quick meal options. This stacked bar chart is horizontally scrollable to explore all areas.

Key Insights

Our analysis reveals critical patterns in Melbourne's food accessibility:

CBD Dominance

The Melbourne CBD has 10x more food venues than average suburban areas, creating a stark accessibility divide.

Inner Suburb Clusters

Carlton, Fitzroy, and South Yarra form secondary hubs of food activity, serving as dining destinations for surrounding areas.

Suburban Challenge

Outer suburbs rely heavily on fast food and takeaway options, with limited fine dining or specialist food venues.

After Dark Access

Late-night food accessibility is severely limited outside central areas, affecting shift workers and night-time travelers.

Data Sources

Analysis Scope
